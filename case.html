A furniture configurator with not a single 3D file

Luna is a site for a fictional sofa brand. At its heart is a 3D configurator where the model, the fabrics and even the catalogue photos are generated by code right in the browser. Here’s how it works.

The problem

Selling a sofa that doesn’t exist yet

Made-to-order furniture is hard to sell online: customers can’t see what their combination of fabric, colour and legs will look like. Photographing every option is impossible — there are well over a million. Typical 3D configurators download dozens of megabytes of models and textures and crawl on a phone.

The solution

Generate everything in the browser

Every part of the sofa is a parametric “soft box” that the code rounds, inflates and finishes with piping. Fabric textures are drawn on a canvas and turned into normal maps. Changing size or fabric means rebuilding geometry in milliseconds instead of downloading a new file.

    The hard parts

    Problems I had to solve

    Seams on a smooth surface

    BoxGeometry faces don’t share vertices, so after rounding, dark lines appeared along the edges. The fix: average the normals of vertices at the same position, and pack vertices densely into the rounded band instead of spreading them evenly.

    Textures without visible tiling

    Procedural bouclé loops and leather grain showed seams at tile edges. Every stroke that touches an edge is also drawn in the wrapped copies of the canvas, so the texture closes on itself.

    AR without a server

    AR needs a model file, and there are no files. The current configuration is exported to GLB right in the browser; model-viewer starts WebXR on Android and generates USDZ for Quick Look on iPhone.

    Smooth on a phone

    Textures are generated once and shared by every scene. Scenes stop rendering when off-screen. Catalogue photos render in a separate context, one per frame, and that context is released straight away.

    Three languages, no framework

    Static copy carries data-i18n keys; dynamic copy goes through t(). Prices, dates and country names come from Intl in the visitor’s locale. A script checks that German and Ukrainian cover every English key.

    Privacy by default

    No cookies, no analytics, no third-party requests: fonts and libraries are self-hosted, which keeps the site GDPR-friendly without a cookie banner. The cart lives in localStorage.

    Details

    The micro-interactions behind the wow

    • The sofa assembles itself: parts drop in one by one and the legs grow
    • Colours blend smoothly and the page background picks up the tint
    • The price counts up and shows the difference, e.g. +€65
    • Clicking a part in 3D opens the matching settings
    • The camera flies to the legs when you change them
    • The configuration lives in the URL, so it’s shareable
    • The cart thumbnail is a real render of your sofa
    • Confetti in the colours of your configuration
